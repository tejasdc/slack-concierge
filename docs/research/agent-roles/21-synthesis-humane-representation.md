# A humane representation of thought, for a mind that works through agents
Synthesis v3, 2026-09-26. Design only, nothing built. Evidence is in files 17–20 plus earlier files.
Built from first principles and ignoring current system constraints, as he asked.

## 0. The question
What representation lets his own mind (not a second brain) hold, grow and act on its thoughts, when
agents do most of the acting? What must he hold himself, and what can the medium and the agents hold?

## 1. Collisions: sources set against each other

### 1a. Who is the mitochondrion? Rao against Tejas
- Rao ("Our Eukaryotic Moment", 2026-09-12) says AI is the nucleus (the compressed genome of culture) and
  humans are mitochondria. Humans supply "liveness": attention, desire, stakes, valuation, taste,
  accountability and contact with reality.
- Tejas's vault note "Agents as Mitochondria" says agents are the mitochondria that power turning his thoughts into reality.
- Lane & Martin (2010, contested in its figures) say mitochondria gave each gene far more energy, so the cell
  could afford a large genome and complexity. The cost was regulation: energy abundance made gene regulation
  the new bottleneck.
- Resolution: each is half right. Agents supply metabolism (energy to express an idea). He supplies liveness
  (which ideas matter). The nucleus is neither of them. It is the shared representation, the durable genome
  of ideas, decisions and reasons that both sides read and express. Mitochondria moved most of their genes
  into the nucleus, where they are protected from the damage of energy production, and kept a small local
  genome for fast local control (CoRR). In the same way, agents must hand what they learn back to the shared
  representation and keep only minimal local state. Sessions compact and die, the way mitochondria get
  damaged; the genome must not live there.
- Consequence: with agents, energy is abundant and regulation is scarce. His decisions are the regulatory
  bottleneck. That is why design threads stall and why he spreads thin. The system should economize his
  decisions, not his typing.

### 1b. Victor against agents; Licklider against Victor
- Victor (Dynamicland 2024 FAQ): "People do not have conversations with their tools." Generative AI is
  "outsourcing understanding in its most virulent form." And: "A book cannot do something for you… reading a
  book can change you into someone who can do something for yourself."
- Licklider (1960): men set goals, and computers do the routinizable work. That is symbiosis.
- The evidence (Bastani 2025; the Anthropic 2026 coding RCT, d=0.74; MIT 2025): harm comes from pure
  delegation. Engaged use protects.
- Resolution: the conversation is the transport, not the medium. Today his representation of a thought IS a
  chat log, and that is the inhumane part. The medium must be a thing he can see and change directly. Agents
  act on it and leave traces in it. He reads the thought, not the chatter about it.

### 1c. The immutable record against reconsolidation
- The system today keeps an append-only history. Nader (2000) showed that recalling a memory makes it labile
  and rewrites it. Complementary learning systems separate a fast episodic store from slowly consolidated
  structure.
- Resolution: two layers. The episodes (captures, conversation) are immutable. The face (the thread's current
  understanding) is rewritten every time the thread is reactivated. Recall is reconsolidation.

### 1d. Nelson against hierarchy research
- Nelson: hierarchy falsifies thought, because everything is intertwingled, and links should be two-way and
  visible. Simon and the organization research: near-decomposable hierarchy is what survives. Bergman:
  people navigate small stable categories better than tag clouds.
- Resolution: a typed graph of threads over a few stable areas. Links carry the intertwingling. Areas give a
  small hierarchy for navigation.

### 1e. Hebbian allocation against misfiling
- Han et al. 2007 and memory linking (Cai 2016): new memories join the currently excitable ensemble, which is
  how events close in time get associated. That is the mechanism behind the Inbox filing a capture into
  whatever thread is "hot", right and wrong.
- Resolution: heat decides links, never merges. Co-activation proposes a related-to link cheaply. A merge
  changes identity and needs him.

### 1f. Pre-named organs against letting them emerge (his own contradiction)
- Self-healing-agents built named sleep and immune layers. The agent-ecology prompt says not to pre-name sleep
  or immunity and to let them evolve.
- Resolution by domain. For his thought medium, the failures are measured (compaction losses, 202 stale
  sessions, misfiles, repeated mistakes), so name the organs that answer them. For agent societies, keep the
  open experiment. Naming is justified by an observed failure, not by the metaphor.

### 1g. Friction against delay aversion
- Bjork's desirable difficulties, Little's law and commitment devices argue for friction. ADHD delay aversion
  and task-initiation difficulty argue against it.
- Resolution: no friction at capture, and none at starting a small action. Friction only at activation past
  the cap, at resting a decision, and at foundation changes. Each friction is one small step with a default.

## 3. What he holds, and what the medium and agents hold
- He holds liveness and structure: what matters (valuation), decisions and their reasons, invariants,
  failure signatures and the shape of how things work. Endsley levels 2–3, and what chunking says experts hold.
- The medium holds the genome: every thread's face, links, trails, decisions and the reasons behind them.
- Agents hold metabolism: execution, retrieval, maintaining faces, consolidation at night, and immune checks.
- Internalization is made to happen, not hoped for. "Since you last looked" on every face. A face explains how
  the thing works, not just what was done. For foundation changes, an optional predict-then-reveal ("what do
  you think this changes?") before the explanation, from the generation effect. Occasionally an explain-it-cold
  prompt on a system he relies on, from the illusion of explanatory depth.

## 4. What changes in the proposal (v3 on top of v2)
1. **Every thread has a face**: one living page, what it is, what's decided and why, what's open, how it works,
   what changed since he last looked. The owner agent maintains it and rewrites it on each reactivation. He can
   edit it directly. The conversation sits underneath as history.
2. **Map and trails**: a spatial view of threads and their typed links (iconic mode). He can drag to link, merge,
   focus or rest (enactive mode). He can replay how a thread grew (Bush's trails). Links are visible from both
   ends (Nelson).
3. **Sleep**, specified as above, with its output ready in the morning as proposals, never silent changes.
4. **Immune function**: repeated mistakes and damage signals produce proposed antibodies (structural checks)
   that he approves. The approval rule is tolerance.
5. **Decision economy**: decisions are surfaced where they bite, small, with a recommended default. Design
   threads keep a decisions/open list on their face.
6. **Genome custody**: agents write learning into faces and the project's lasting documents. No knowledge lives
   only in a session.

## 5. Honest limits
- Every biological mapping is an analogy. Its value is in predicting designs (reconsolidation → mutable face;
  CoRR → minimal local state; danger theory → damage-triggered checks). It proves nothing.
- Lane & Martin's energy-per-gene figures are contested. The regulation-bottleneck point is an inference.
- The "metabolism of software" article he remembers was not found in his library.
- "Chandler" is most likely Mitch Kapor's Chandler information manager, in the lineage of Lotus Agenda
  (free-form items that categorize themselves), at about 65% confidence. To confirm with him.
- Victor rejects conversing with agents outright. This design keeps agents but moves the medium away from the
  conversation. Victor would still object.
