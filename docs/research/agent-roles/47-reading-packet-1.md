# Reading packet 1: how should a thought be represented?
Primary sources only, with no summaries. Pick one or two per sitting. Note what surprises you and what you
disagree with.

## A. Niklas Luhmann, "Communicating with Slip Boxes" (1981), ~20 minutes
https://luhmann.surge.sh/communicating-with-slip-boxes
Luhmann wrote about 70 books from a box of linked cards, not folders.
1. Why does he give each card a fixed number instead of filing it under a topic? What does that allow that a
   folder doesn't?
2. He calls the box "an alter ego with whom we can constantly communicate". What does the box do to *him* as a
   thinker, beyond storing things? Does it pass your "leaves me better even without the tool" test?
3. Where does surprise come from in his system, and what would kill it?

## B. Bret Victor, "The Humane Representation of Thought" (2014 talk, ~1 hour; good for listening)
https://dynamicland.org/2014/The_Humane_Representation_of_Thought/ (transcript alongside the video)
1. What does he mean by "humane", and what is "inhumane" about today's representations?
2. He draws on three ways of knowing, through action, images and words (Bruner). Which of the three do your
   threads use today? Which would a humane thread use?
3. Would a chat log count as a representation of thought by his definition? Why or why not?

## C. The counter-case: why people keep using folders (read the abstracts yourself)
- Bergman et al., "Improved search engines and navigation preference in personal information management"
  (2008): people navigated folders for 56–68% of retrievals and searched for only 4–15%, even with good search.
  https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1402256.1402259
- Benn et al., "Navigating through digital folders uses the same brain structures as real world navigation"
  (Scientific Reports, 2015). https://www.nature.com/articles/srep14719
1. If folder navigation uses the brain's spatial-navigation system, what is a folder giving people that a web
   of links might take away?
2. Is your objection to *hierarchy*, to *fixed location*, or to *having one place for a thing*? They are
   different claims.
3. What would a representation need so that you could *navigate* your ideas the way you move through a place?

## D. Ted Nelson, one-liners (5 minutes)
https://xanadu.com.au/ted/TN/WRITINGS/TCOMPARADIGM/tedCompOneLiners.html
1. Which line best names your own complaint about files and folders?
2. Nelson wanted links visible from both ends and quotations that stay connected to their source. What would
   that change about how one of your threads relates to another?
