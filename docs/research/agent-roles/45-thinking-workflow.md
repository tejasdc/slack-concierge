# How we work from here: he thinks, the agent supports
Grounded in file 44: Buçinca 2021; Bastani 2025; Anthropic 2026; Dunlosky 2013; Chi 1989/1994; Bangert-Drowns
2004; Rozenblit & Keil 2002; Jansson & Smith 1991 and its 2024 AI replication; Adler's syntopical reading.

## Is this session outsourcing?
By the evidence, yes, mostly. The agent did the reading, the synthesis and five design versions, and he read
the results. That pattern is "delegate then accept": fluent explanations raise overreliance and give an
illusion of understanding. Seeing a candidate design first also narrows his own ideas (design fixation). The
atlas and designs are therefore a map of what exists, not conclusions to adopt.

## The loop (one question per sitting)
1. **His question, his words.** He states what he's trying to understand or decide, by voice or text.
2. **His answer first.** He predicts or sketches his answer before any agent material. It's allowed to be
   rough. (Committing first reduces overreliance and protects his own ideas.)
3. **One primary source, firsthand.** The agent picks the one or two sources that carry the most weight and
   prepares them: the original text or talk, with page or section pointers and 3 questions. No summary. He
   notes what surprises him and what he disagrees with.
4. **He explains it back.** The agent asks why, how, what would break it, and how it connects to something he
   already knows. The agent does not supply the answer. It pushes back when he's too quick.
5. **He writes the synthesis or decision.** The agent checks it against the sources, points out gaps and
   counter-evidence, and records the alternatives he rejected, in his words. This is the dialog record.
6. **Test later.** A day later, the agent asks him to explain it cold, predict a case, or defend it against an
   objection. Where he stumbles is where to look next.

## Who does what
- **Him:** asks the questions, commits first, reads the load-bearing sources, explains, writes, decides.
- **Agent:**
  - fetches and prepares sources;
  - asks questions and challenges;
  - keeps the record (CoDIAK streams: sources = intelligence, this dialog = records, his synthesis = the product);
  - checks his understanding;
  - withholds its own synthesis unless he explicitly asks, and even then only after his step 2.
- **Subagents:** evidence gathering the agent names. Never synthesis on his behalf.

## How much to read firsthand
- Read firsthand the sources that decide something, or that he'll cite as his reason: a few per question.
- Use summaries only to decide what to read.
- Summaries, rereading and highlighting are low-utility techniques (Dunlosky 2013); self-explanation and
  generating come out much stronger.

## ADHD fit
- One question and one source per sitting.
- Voice counts as generation.
- Short loops that end with something written by him: a sentence is enough.
- The record lets him stop and resume without holding the loop open in his head.
